# Before/after editorial comparison — 2026-09-22

**Method and limitation:** the same author reviewed the preserved pre-extension core/references and the candidate on the same prompts. “Before” below describes the existing library's documented support, not a weaker model answer invented for comparison. “After” specimens are editorial demonstrations written during review, not fresh-context model outputs. No criterion scores, retrieval traces, performance gains, or independent acceptance are claimed. A ten-case frozen suite is available for actual evaluation; acceptance-status.json records it as unrun.

## 1. From scattered observations to a discriminating interpretation

**Shared prompt:** Three unrelated clinics advertise AI scribes; two staff interviews praise speed; a vendor says everyone wants fully automated consultations. Form a testable opportunity and a credible rival interpretation.

**Before:** Factfulness and the existing core already reject a dramatic generalization from selected cases and separate capability from deployment and benefit. They can ask for denominators and missing complements. The core does not explicitly teach forming a candidate behavioral pattern from disparate signals, separating curation stages, or constructing a rival that explains the same observations.

**After specimen:** “The supported observation is interest in reducing documentation burden in a few settings. One interpretation is that staff want more attention available for patients; a credible rival is that vendors are responding to temporary promotion or incentives rather than durable workflow demand. Neither supports a universal preference for automated consultations. An overlooked opportunity is a documentation service that preserves or improves the consultation itself. Compare repeat use after introductory support ends, staff work avoided versus shifted into correction, and patient understanding in a defined clinic sample. Include clinics that tried and declined the tools. These observations could distinguish durable workflow value from promotion-driven uptake.”

**Added support:** Megatrends' Gathering–Aggregating–Elevating distinction and qualified Proving; Thinking's story questions; the new core's rival-interpretation test. The improvement sought is a better hypothesis and observation plan, not a longer trend list.

## 2. From output gains to an opportunity with a beneficiary and complements

**Shared prompt:** A team rewards AI proposal volume. Output rises, clients report generic proposals, and juniors stop attending discovery calls. Revise the intervention.

**Before:** The original human-capacity section and We Are as Gods already diagnose higher output with weaker understanding and propose changing the division of work. The existing final-action section already asks for owner, review point, and continue/change/stop conditions. These are retained strengths, not newly invented capabilities.

**After specimen:** “The volume reward is selecting for quick generic submissions and displacing the conversations that teach staff what clients need. Test an assisted-proposal service in one team for a small set of comparable engagements. Keep a named proposal owner; restore staff participation in discovery and use AI for bounded drafting and critique. The beneficiary is the client receiving a better-fitted proposal and the team developing reusable judgment. Required complements include discovery time, access to client context, competent review, and a reward tied to fit rather than count. The decisive uncertainty is whether assistance frees effort for understanding or mainly creates more correction. Review client-assessed fit, rework, and staff ability to explain the recommendation unaided. Continue if fit and understanding improve within the agreed effort budget; revise if work shifts without benefit; stop the volume incentive if it keeps rewarding generic output.”

**Added support:** Future Normal's job/role recomposition; Thinking's problem framing and Option C; Out of Control's selection environment. The concrete measures are proposed editorial tests, not claims that the books tested this team. A workflow where participants can modify or decline particular uses preserves practical choice.

## 3. Network participation without confusing collective value with capture

**Shared prompt:** A cooperative repair network can share diagnostics freely, but one platform proposes exclusive customer ownership in exchange for matching demand. How should a small repair shop participate?

**Before:** The original agency section, Azhar, and What Technology Wants already ask who controls access, captures gains, and faces switching costs. The original Inevitable reference supplies generatives and stewardship. A fair review cannot credit the extension with discovering dependence for the first time.

**After specimen:** “Shared diagnostics can reduce duplicated diagnosis and make more repairs feasible; matching can connect customers to the shop with the right skills and parts. That value depends on useful contributors, accurate information, compatible records, and reasons to keep contributing. The shop's return is separate: exclusive customer ownership could route future demand through an operator that can change fees or access. Test a limited arrangement with clear contribution and payment terms, bounded exclusivity if any, and a practical route to retain permitted records and serve customers independently. Compare suitable completed jobs and net earnings after platform, coordination, and rework costs. Expand only if the additional value remains accessible to the shop; revise or leave if dependence rises faster than benefits.”

**Added support:** all ten New Rules strategies, especially Increasing Returns, Follow the Free, Feed the Web First, and Relationship Tech, paired with existing platform-power analysis. Neither node counts nor network growth alone establishes the shop's profit. The trial terms are a design proposal, not legal advice or a claim of source consensus.

## 4. Revise an intervention when local responses defeat its purpose

**Shared prompt:** Our company wants to remove all safety sign-off because bee swarms coordinate without managers. Is this a valid application of distributed intelligence?

**Before:** The existing core already says a commons needs stewardship, institutions may protect functions worth retaining, and irreversible harm can require more evidence. It rejects a simple inference from technological tendencies to a particular project choice.

**After specimen:** “The analogy is insufficient. Identify which teams hold local information, what they can reliably observe, and which errors would reach other people before correction. Local experimentation may improve a bounded process, while cross-team hazards and consequential commitments still need accountable decisions. Preserve sign-off where it performs that job; test whether a particular approval can be delegated with explicit criteria, recorded outcomes, and escalation for uncertainty. If local optimization increases defects or hides them until later, change the selection rule or restore coordination. Continue only where evidence supports both the intended outcome and containment of failures.”

**Added support:** Out of Control's paired swarm disadvantages, metacontrol, and selection logic; New Rules' paralysis-without-direction counterexample. On its own this case tests inapplicability and governance. Case 2 supplies the direct before/after comparison on participants adapting to an incentive and the resulting intervention revision.
